TMRL:

Diffusion Timestep-Modulated Pretraining Enables Exploration for Efficient Policy Finetuning

Real-world reinforcement learning with a VLA — in under one hour.

TL;DR

A unified framework bridging behavior-cloning pre-training and reinforcement-learning fine-tuning.

↓

Problem

BC bottlenecks RL

In unseen contexts, the conditional p(a | c) collapses. BC assigns near-zero probability to optimal actions, leaving RL without a gradient signal.

⇌

Approach

Context Smoothing + Timestep Modulation

CSP noises the context to interpolate between p(a | c) and p(a), aliasing unseen contexts back in-distribution. TMRL dials σ per action chunk.

◷

Result

Real-world RL in < 1 hr

With a context-smoothed π0, TMRL reaches near-perfect success on three real-robot tasks: under an hour on WidowX, under four on Franka.

Motivation

The need for action distribution interpolation.

Robots trained by imitation copy demonstrations narrowly — they fail in unfamiliar situations, and reinforcement learning can’t recover them because the imitation policy starts so confidently wrong that there’s no learning signal. We deliberately blur what the policy “knows” so RL can find improvements within an hour of real-robot time.

On a 2D pointmaze from OGBench, we train two policies — one approximating the conditional p(a | c), the other the marginal p(a) — where c is the goal position. Both train on pointmaze-large and evaluate on the larger, out-of-distribution pointmaze-giant with an unseen goal (agent in yellow, goal in pink).

OOD goal — left: p(a | c) collapses and drifts away. Right: p(a) covers broadly enough to reach.

The conditional collapses on optimal actions; the marginal covers them only with low likelihood. We need a policy that interpolates between the two — and a mechanism to learn where on that interpolation to operate.

Method

Context smoothing & timestep modulation.

≈

Pre-training · CSP

Context-Smoothed Policy

For each pair (c, a): sample σ, draw c ~ qσ(c | c), then optimize pθ(a | c, σ). Training across σ ∈ [0, T] yields a single weight set covering the full conditional → marginal spectrum.

◎

Fine-tuning · TMRL

Timestep-Modulated RL

Freeze pθ. Run off-policy RL over πHL(z, σ | s). Large σ drives structured exploration beyond the conditional support; small σ recovers precise imitation. The choice is made per chunk — modulation happens within a single rollout.

Diffusion noise on pointcloud contexts

On the dexterous-grasping suite, the context c is the object pointcloud. As σ rises, qσ progressively corrupts c with Gaussian noise: the pointcloud slowly loses its original structure, aliasing the out-of-distribution object with nearby in-distribution pointclouds seen during training. The policy can then borrow coherent grasps from related training contexts rather than fail on an unfamiliar shape.

σ Slider

The conditional ↔ marginal spectrum visualized.

As σ rises, qσ(c | c) spreads mass over nearby contexts and the policy borrows coherent action chunks rather than dithering. The endpoints anchor the spectrum: σ = 0 recovers p(a | c), σ = T recovers p(a). On unseen contexts the conditional collapses to a narrow distribution that leaves RL with no reward signal — TMRL learns where on this spectrum to operate.

Key insight

The perturbation lives in context space, not action space. Action diversity grows with σ while individual chunks stay coherent — CSP borrows action sequences from nearby contexts rather than jittering the current one.

Simulation Results

Coverage and RL sample efficiency.

Action coverage before RL: Success@K

Success@K — the fraction of out-of-distribution states where at least one of K base-policy rollouts succeeds — directly measures action coverage. On pointmaze-giant and cube-single, CSP exceeds PostBC and BC at every K, with the gap widest on cube-single where both baselines remain at zero.
